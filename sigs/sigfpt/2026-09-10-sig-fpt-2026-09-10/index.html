SIG-FPT 2026-09-10

Participants: Venkatesh Rao, Andre Comeau, Aneesh Sathe, Florian Lohse (UTC+0), Sean Stevenson

The group did a round of reactions to a paper on reductionism vs. constructionism (emergence, symmetry, one-way interpretations of systems), connecting it to Dennett on evolution and path dependence, and to a live debate about OpenAI's claimed Navier–Stokes results and Terence Tao's critique of "solution extraction." A recurring thread was whether the *value* lies in a final answer/prophecy or in the scaffolding and world-models built along the way. The session closed with roughly the last quarter devoted to planning an upcoming symposium/special session featuring three talks plus a discussion block.

  • **Andre:** Found the paper a good argument but noted it made him *uncomfortable*, suspecting it exposed an overly reductionist view of the universe he needs to work through.
  • **Aneesh (emergence, memes & AI):** Riffed on emergence via a "psycho-history" angle — memes let us build small cultures at the individual level, but with AI "memes have escaped the need for minds." AI-driven memes/"micro machines" (the "word machines" idea) can interact independently of people, and history/psycho-history could emerge from that interplay at a higher (quasi-mesoscale, grand-historical) level.
  • **Aneesh (biology as physics):** Views biology as physics expressing itself, occupying every available niche where an energy differential allows life to extract energy — so given the right conditions, life is nearly inevitable.
  • **Florian (reductionism ≠ constructionism):** Connected "a reductionist hypothesis does not license a constructionist hypothesis" to Musk-style first-principles engineering (e.g. rockets) — you identify guiding principles then rebuild the whole system; he has used this himself but always found it slightly uncomfortable, and thinks this may be why. Also flagged the paper's notion of **symmetry** (a system appearing the same from different viewpoints) as intriguing but not yet fully digested, and tied the paper's "one-way interpretations of systems" to Dennett's claim that you can't replay evolution and get the same result.
  • **Sean (contained sciences & morality):** Reading the paper convinced him that with enough abstraction, biological/evolutionary theory can be a self-contained science not dependent on lower layers. Was pleasantly surprised by the humility of Dennett's treatment of morality at the end of *Darwin's Dangerous Idea*, which opens with Hume's objection (you can't get an *ought* from an *is*); Sean suggested the "ought" lives at a higher level (social sciences) in the hierarchy of sciences.
  • **Venkatesh (Dennett & path dependence):** Argued (about halfway through the book) that it's Stephen Jay Gould, not Dennett, who over-emphasizes path dependence. Dennett occupies a midpoint between chaotic sensitivity (butterflies causing hurricanes) and a Nietzschean eternal-recurrence hell of exact repetition: evolutionary histories are sensitive to initial/path conditions but not *radically* divergent — "forced moves" (eyes, bodily symmetry) recur, so something recognizably close to us would re-emerge even if specific Homo sapiens details differ.
