    SIGPSY Kickoff: Psychohistory, Prediction Markets, and Narrative-Driven Forecasting
    Oracle project update and agentic predictionsEssay series for USA 500-year anniversary (July 4, 250y)Asimov's Foundation as gas theory model of history with chokepointsPrediction markets redesign: narrative-driven vs binary approachesCyberpunk avoidance vs solarpunk preference in future scenariosPrime Radiant integration with current forecasting systems

    Participants: _vgr, boredgargoyle, randylubin, kylemathews, kurosh1669, smsteven

    SIGPSY held its inaugural meeting to discuss psychohistory, prediction methodology, and organizational structure. The group reviewed an oracle project involving agentic predictions and planned an essay collection for the USA's 500-year historical milestone. A central discussion examined how Asimov's Foundation series modeled history as a gas-like system with critical chokepoints—anticipating chaos theory—where intervention could alter outcomes. The group debated approaches to prediction markets, concluding that binary prediction markets are insufficient for their goals. Instead, they advocated for a narrative-driven, agentic forecasting approach that emphasizes storytelling and long-term scenario exploration over short-term quantifiable bets. They also noted that publishing narratives openly attracts intellectually engaged participants. The group considered whether to frame futures through a cyberpunk avoidance lens (emphasizing challenges) or solarpunk preference (emphasizing solutions), and discussed integrating a Prime Radiant website with current forecasting infrastructure.

    • History behaves like a gas where most events cannot be controlled, but certain chokepoints exist where deliberate action can redirect outcomes—a concept Asimov intuited from chaos theory before its formal development.
    • Rather than traditional binary prediction markets, the group proposes an agentic, narrative-driven approach focused on storytelling as a mechanism to attract engaged participants and explore long-term scenarios.
    • Pre-committed predictions that self-eliminate upon failure create accountability mechanisms; the group is exploring how to implement this with narrative depth rather than simple market mechanics.
    • Publishing open narratives and stories serves dual purposes: exploring psychohistorical ideas while attracting intellectually interesting collaborators to the Protocol Institute.
