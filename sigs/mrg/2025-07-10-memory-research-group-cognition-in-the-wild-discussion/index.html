Memory Research Group: Cognition in the Wild Discussion

Edwin Hutchins' theory of distributed cognition and permeable boundaries between individual and systemOverlapping memories across individual and system levelsFormal modeling limitations in capturing system operationsDon Norman's distinction between knowledge in the mind versus knowledge in the worldMental models and cognitive artifacts

Participants: keikreutler, timber1997, kakooza_vian, yatu2168, thewanderingeditor, bhaumik, guymyguy

The Memory Research Group convened to discuss excerpts from Edwin Hutchins' 'Cognition in the Wild' (1995), focusing on pages 175-180, 195-196, and 359-364. The session explored Hutchins' theory of distributed cognition, particularly his conception of the permeable boundary between individual cognition and larger systems. Participants noted that Hutchins highlights how memories and cognitive processes overlap and are shared between individuals and systems, challenging traditional boundaries of what constitutes individual versus collective knowledge. The discussion touched on limitations in Hutchins' formal model, specifically its potential inability to distinguish between different operational types occurring within systems themselves. One participant drew connections to Don Norman's complementary framework distinguishing between knowledge stored in the mind versus knowledge accessible in the world, suggesting these theoretical perspectives address similar questions about how cognition extends beyond individual minds into artifacts and systems.

  • Hutchins emphasizes a permeable boundary between individual cognition and larger systems, with memories distributed across both levels rather than cleanly separated.
  • Hutchins' formal model may not adequately distinguish between different types of operations occurring within the system itself, leaving a gap in the theoretical framework.
  • Don Norman's concept of knowledge in the world as external cognitive support parallels Hutchins' ideas about how cognition extends beyond individual minds into environmental and systemic structures.
