Memory Research Group Session - Michael Levin's Bioelectric Memory

Bioelectric networks and non-genetic patterning informationThe space of possible mindsRegeneration and development mechanismsMemory storage in biological systemsMichael Levin's research on morphogenesis

Participants: keikreutler

The Memory Research Group held its third meeting on July 24, 2025, focusing on Michael Levin's research on bioelectric memory in biological systems. The session involved co-reading two key materials: Levin's Noema article "The Space Of Possible Minds" and a summary of his 2021 paper on endogenous bioelectric networks. These readings form the foundation for exploring how biological systems store and utilize non-genetic patterning information during regeneration and development.

The session examined Levin's groundbreaking work demonstrating that bioelectric networks function as information storage systems beyond traditional genetic mechanisms. This research challenges conventional views of memory as exclusively neurological or genetic, suggesting that cellular-level bioelectric patterns encode and preserve developmental information. The group's focus on this third session indicates ongoing investigation into the intersection of memory, biological organization, and the potential diversity of cognitive and information-processing systems in nature.

  • Bioelectric networks store patterning information during regeneration and development independent of genetic sequences, suggesting alternative mechanisms for biological memory.
  • Michael Levin's work explores the conceptual space of possible minds, challenging conventional understanding of where and how memory emerges in living systems.
  • Non-genetic information storage through bioelectric signaling represents a fundamental mechanism for maintaining and transmitting biological patterns across cellular and organismal scales.
