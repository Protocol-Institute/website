SIG-MRG 2026-08-13

Participants: 🙊 Jenna Dixon, Kei 🗝, neilson, ayseayse__, Othmangba, Aaron Z Lewis, Mark Elder, Matthew Bright UTC-7, Pratik, Sachin

This was the first of what may be several sessions on Stiegler. Kei (the facilitator) framed the difficulty of the text — not conceptually hard so much as built from "references to references" — and provided a summary document plus a glossary (made by Mark) as entry points. After an opening round-robin prompt about participants' oldest possessions and how those objects "remember" them, the group spent a silent reading block, then discussed Stiegler's central reframing of technology as the *mutual invention* of human and tool, the distinction between genetic/epigenetic/tertiary memory, and how this compares to other philosophies of technology.

  • **Opening prompt (oldest possession / how it remembers you):** Participants shared childhood teddy bears (neilson's "teddy," Othmangba's stuffed orca "Shamu"), first books (ayseayse), a Sega Master System (Mark), a grandfather-built house (Matthew), a father's watch (Pratik), a red suitcase carried when emigrating from India (Sachin), and — as a hypothetical — Aaron's story of a memory-stone that grew *more* powerful after being lost. Kei's example was a fraying childhood teddy ("Puff"), which prompted his question: does the object remember *you* specifically, or just some generic human interaction?
  • **Kei's central reading of Stiegler:** The text flips Leroi-Gourhan's idea of technology-as-exteriorization-of-memory into a *mutual invention* — humans did not simply invent technology; technology co-invented the human, in a feedback loop. The earliest marks on flint are, in this view, simultaneously the first linguistic exteriority *and* the thing that produced human interiority. The line between interior and exterior is therefore vague; interiority may only be constituted by the reflexivity of the exterior.
  • **Three orders of memory:** Kei laid out Stiegler's schema — genetic (primary), epigenetic (secondary), and **tertiary memory** (technological). Tertiary memory allows the knowledge of one lifetime to be passed on *non-genetically* across generations via the tool, the mark, the photograph. It functions like an "impersonal epigenetics": you needn't know the person who carved the flint, yet with enough context you can pick up the mantle of their memory. Kei noted Stiegler seems hesitant to push this all the way.
  • **Sachin — agency and stigmergy:** With epigenetic memory you have essentially no control over transmission, but with inherited *tools* (a "stigmergic" framing — science built on science) you have more agency to influence the next interpretation. He also linked this to TV as a shaping technology, referencing Kei's Substack post on TV as "synthetic data" and a David Foster Wallace essay on how television shaped fiction writing — a large corpus of synthetic influence that leaves a trace even on people not directly exposed.
  • **Othmangba — feedback loops:** Related the human–technology co-development to the reciprocal shaping of culture and institutions (institutions shape culture and vice versa). Earlier he also connected the theme to generational trauma and to how technology reshapes learned behaviors (e.g., a Zoom call triggering fight-or-flight).
  • **ayseayse — LLMs and traces:** Connected the reading to "there is no outside of the text" and Husserl's phenomenological notion of retention; argued meaning emerges collectively through continuous interaction rather than within fixed personal "boundaries," since externalization and interiorization are constantly in play. She is writing a paper drawing parallels between LLMs and Stiegler's ideas.
