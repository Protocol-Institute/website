SIGPfB Optional Discussion: Time Estimation Chaos & Protocol Development Approach

Time Estimation Chaos problem revisited from basicsFat vs. Lean business structure concepts and adoption barriersProtocol definition and solution discussion methodologyPBR (Protocol-Based Reference) series format and executionVisual communication standards for diagrams and documentationCase study development as prioritized next step

Participants: rafa_0x, drevius., timber1997, sachbenny

Rafa opened the discussion by proposing a return to basics on the Time Estimation Chaos topic to enable meaningful analysis and protocol development. Drevius responded with a pragmatic framework emphasizing that item 3 (tension definition and protocol solutions) offers the best return on investment, while the Fat vs. Lean concept hasn't yet resonated. He advocated for producing small, practical tools through terse, dense documentation rather than exploring broad conceptual worldviews. Drevius offered to create a cohesive visual design system (consistent colors and fonts) for the PBR series, similar to what was done for the tensions article, to ensure professional consistency across outputs.

The group converged on prioritizing case study development and the PBR medium format as the near-term focus, with the belief that this concrete work will naturally reveal the longer-term strategy without needing to specify it upfront. Timber added a note about being willing to eliminate ideas that don't serve the work—a principle borrowed from fiction writing—suggesting a disciplined approach to scope management. The overall tone emphasized moving from abstract discussion toward actionable, well-crafted outputs.

  • The group should return to foundational concepts around Time Estimation Chaos to enable deeper understanding and discussion, rather than building on incomplete prior work.
  • Drevius advocates for practical, small-scale tools over broad worldview changes, and emphasizes that tight, well-scoped outputs (terse prose and dense diagrams) will produce stronger results than meandering discussions.
  • A consistent visual design system (color palette, fonts, diagram style) should be established for the PBR series to ensure coherent documentation across case studies.
  • Focusing on nailing case studies and the PBR medium first will organically reveal what to do with the work long-term, making upfront specification of distant goals unnecessary.
  • The group should be willing to cut ideas that don't serve the core work, following a principle from fiction writing of 'killing your darlings' when needed.
