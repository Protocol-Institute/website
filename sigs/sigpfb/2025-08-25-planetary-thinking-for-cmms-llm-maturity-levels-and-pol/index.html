Planetary Thinking for CMMs: LLM Maturity Levels and Political Frameworks

Five-level maturity framework for LLMs (from toys to planetary intelligence)Politics of AI adoption across different maturity levelsHistorical analogies (containerization, nuclear energy, cloud computing) for technology maturationCommons versus monopoly dynamics in LLM development and governanceShadow AI adoption in organizations and policy implicationsLegal personhood and entity status for AI systems at advanced maturity levels

Participants: sachbenny, rafa_0x, timber1997, drevius.

The SIGPfB group applied Yuk Hui's philosophical framework to understand how Large Language Models mature through five distinct levels, from toys to planetary-scale intelligence infrastructure. Rather than treating LLM adoption as a monolithic phenomenon, participants mapped different political dynamics at each level: personal copyright anxieties (Level 1), union-style institutional resistance (Level 2), standards wars and regulation battles (Level 3), competitive adoption cycles (Level 4), and finally planetary infrastructure requiring reimagined governance (Level 5). The discussion drew parallels to historical technologies like shipping containers, nuclear energy, and cloud computing to illustrate how tools eventually become ambient infrastructure that reshape business, law, and geopolitics.

A key tension emerged around whether Level 5 maturity could support commons-based models or inevitably tends toward monopoly extraction. Participants noted that market incentives create opposing pressures—lagging organizations will push for nationalized or commons LLMs while leaders seek monopolistic control. The group also highlighted current organizational fragility: 44% of employees are already using AI tools without authorization, uploading sensitive data to public platforms, suggesting that institutions haven't yet adapted governance models to match actual adoption patterns. This gap may presage the Level 3-4 transitions where regulatory compliance and institutional politics dominate.

The group concluded that the containerization analogy deserves deeper exploration at a follow-up meeting, and that understanding these maturity levels is essential for predicting how businesses will need to restructure as LLMs evolve from discrete tools toward embedded, autonomous entities with potential legal personhood at the highest maturity stages.

  • LLMs progress through distinct maturity levels with different political manifestations: Level 1 (personal politics, copyright debates) → Level 5 (planetary infrastructure requiring new governance models and potentially legal entity status).
  • Technology adoption is non-linear across domains; self-driving cars already function at infrastructure level (Level 4-5) while business copywriting remains at tool stage (Level 2), suggesting different regulatory and business approaches are needed simultaneously.
  • At Level 5 maturity, LLMs may become so embedded in infrastructure that they require legal frameworks similar to those proposed for rivers and natural entities, with organizations becoming their own digitized entities that users interface with directly.
  • Market incentives create opposing pressures: lagging players will advocate for commons-based LLMs while leaders push for monopolistic control, potentially triggering LLM nationalization similar to nuclear energy or GPS.
  • Current unauthorized employee AI use (44% of workers uploading sensitive data to public platforms) reveals a critical gap between organizational policy and actual adoption, foreshadowing Level 3-4 institutional tensions.
