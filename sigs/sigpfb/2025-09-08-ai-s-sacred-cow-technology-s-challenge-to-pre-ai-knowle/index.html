AI's Sacred Cow: Technology's Challenge to Pre-AI Knowledge Work

Sacred cows slain by technology adoption across domains (spreadsheets, email, procedural generation, air conditioning)Shift from effort-based to editorial work: production becomes cheap, curation becomes valuableCraftmanship and power dynamics as central to resistance against disruptive technologiesQuality signals dilution when low-resolution output becomes trivial to produceRole transformation from 'doing the work' to 'choosing among options'

Participants: timber1997, sachbenny, rafa_0x, drevius., stevebeans., amitashu, xxaudemarsxx, kpats, _vgr

The SIGPfB group explored which core commitments of pre-AI knowledge work are most directly challenged by AI adoption—what constitutes AI's 'sacred cow.' Through case studies spanning spreadsheets, PowerPoint, procedural generation in games, and even air conditioning, participants identified recurring patterns: technologies systematically overturn beliefs about what requires human effort, specialized skill, or centralized control. The group observed that sacred cows often intertwine with craftmanship, professional identity, and power dynamics. In finance, spreadsheets inverted the bottleneck from recalculation to decision-making; in presentation software, visual design shifted from expensive specialists to democratized capability.

For current AI adoption, participants identified a critical tension: when low-resolution thinking becomes trivially cheap to produce, quality signals based on effort dilute. This creates psychological resistance framed as 'cheating,' even as throughput and potential quality improve. The conversation converged on a work transformation: knowledge jobs are shifting from 'doing the work' to 'choosing among options'—from production toward curation and editorial refinement. Participants noted that scanning and synthesis time increases 5x with LLM use, but actual composition accelerates dramatically, elevating the value of broad contextual knowledge and judgment over apprenticeship time.

  • Technology adoption patterns show consistent inversion: static→fluid, central power→individual execution, production→editorializing. Sacred cows are often tied to power dynamics and perceived craftmanship.
  • AI adoption presents a barrier: mediocre output is now on-tap, making effort an unreliable signal of quality. This creates psychological resistance ('using this technology is cheating') even as it enables higher throughput.
  • Post-LLM knowledge work rewards scanning, synthesis, and evaluation skills over rote production. Broad reading and contextual judgment become more valuable than time spent on execution.
  • The work transformation manifests as bimodal: either workers become editors/curators of AI output, or they face commodification. The 'job' shifts from producing work to detecting and refining AI-generated options.
