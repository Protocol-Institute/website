Protocols for Business: Protocol Fiction Aesthetics and AI-Native Organization

Etymology and origins of business terminology (vertical integration)Protocol fiction aesthetics and visual metaphors for complex systemsTerraforming as unifying metaphor for factories, rooms, and agent spacesAccessibility and communication strategy for mainstream audiencesAI-native companies and new technological layer of natural laws

Participants: rafa_0x, _vgr, sachbenny, timber1997, drwip, tomguarriello

The SIGPfB group discussed the etymology of business terminology and explored why certain terms may not make intuitive sense upon scrutiny. The conversation then shifted to developing better communication frameworks for their complex protocol work. A key proposal emerged around using 'terraforming' as a unifying visual metaphor to explain various organizational concepts like factories, rooms, and agent spaces in more accessible ways.

The group acknowledged a critical challenge: modern audiences have limited attention spans and are unlikely to read detailed reports, so content must be optimized for both human tweet-length consumption and LLM processing. They introduced 'New Nature' as a branding initiative aimed at mainstream audiences, which frames technology and AI protocols as creating an entirely new layer of natural laws. The closing discussion connected these ideas to the concept of AI-native companies operating within this new technological landscape, aligning with conclusions from international distributed AI workshops.

  • The term 'vertical integration' may derive from physical factory architecture (steam engine shafts) rather than supply chain concepts, though Ford's mines-to-cars integration is likely the actual origin.
  • A 'terraforming' visual metaphor was proposed to unify discussions of factories, rooms, and agent spaces as a more accessible framework for explaining complex protocols.
  • Content needs to be LLM-friendly and designed for non-readers, as modern attention spans are limited to tweet-length content, though the underlying reports are substantive.
  • The group is developing a 'New Nature' branding strategy to make their work accessible to mainstream audiences, positioning technology-created AI protocols as a new layer of natural laws.
  • AI-native companies and products represent a post-adoption reality where artificial beings operate within this new technological nature, similar to conclusions from distributed AI workshops.
