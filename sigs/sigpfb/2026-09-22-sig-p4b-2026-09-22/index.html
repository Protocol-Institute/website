SIG-P4B 2026-09-22

Participants: rafa (UTC+1), Sachin, doxxed_as_zak, Eric Alston (UTC - 6), PAtwater

This session was part of an AI/vibe-coding workshop in which participants demonstrated and discussed self-built software tools. rafa demonstrated a distributed robot-hiring/contracting project and a teleoperated robot, then rafa and Sachin worked through their emerging vocabulary for what they call "factories" (personal, protocolized AI-enabled software that isn't quite a product). The conversation broadened into a recurring theme raised by doxxed_as_zak — the lack of shared language across tools and projects (a "Tower of Babel" problem) — before turning to logistics for an afternoon show-and-tell session and a closing exchange with Eric Alston on how "writing for an external audience" improves both human and AI output.

  • rafa demonstrated Yak Robot (yakrobot.com): distributed robot hiring/contracting on blockchain rails. A person submits an RFP/task in English, it's converted to machine-readable tasks, robots review against their capabilities, bid, win, get paid, execute, and return data. rafa said this "works ish" on mainnet.
  • rafa is also building an LLM data pipeline that converts English PDFs into machine-readable robot tasks (e.g. "take the beer out of the fridge" → specific location/action).
  • rafa distinguishes a "factory" from a product/platform: it's a heavily protocolized process built for oneself or friends, not for commercial scale. Framing something as a "product" pulls you into a different design paradigm.
  • Sachin: a factory is essentially "an extension of a single person" — embodied skills turned into software that can run without them, plus affordances like automation, densification, and bespoke creation (concepts from the prior day).
  • rafa: these are objects that "shouldn't exist" under the old paradigm — previously not cost-effective in time, money, or skill, now possible. They're more than a toy, hardened enough to build a life/community on, but not at scale and not quite open-source software.
  • doxxed_as_zak likened this to the 1980s home-electronics/component-store movement — hobbyist building that later disappeared as products matured.
