Participants: Sachin, rafa (UTC+1), Javier Chavez / UTC -6, 🙊Andre Comeau

The session used the QWERTY keyboard case to explore path dependence, non-ergodic outcomes, and the "sufficiency" of suboptimal-but-good-enough general-purpose technologies. Discussion turned to whether QWERTY's lock-in argument still holds today given voice dictation, LLMs, and touchscreens. The latter half shifted to group logistics: networking/weak-ties initiatives, a possible rebrand away from "Protocols for Business," and a water-industry pro bono report on data discovery.

  • **Sachin (general-purpose tech & convergence):** For a general-purpose technology, it's hard to call outcomes non-ergodic because such technologies are vast and distributed and tend to converge on an equilibrium or a couple of dominant solutions.
  • **rafa (sufficiency of suboptimal tech):** Linked this to a prior discussion of the "unreasonable sufficiency of protocols" — general-purpose technologies (scissors, paper clips) don't need to be optimal; a "lowest level of functionality" that is good enough gets embedded in society and persists. He speculated there may be a rule (perhaps provable) that adopted standards are always magnitudes suboptimal relative to potential, yet aren't displaced — only "liberated."
  • **Javier (hardware and incentives to standardize):** Wondered whether a standard that lasts must precede hardware; noted the QWERTY setup and drew an analogy to a recently released OpenAI hardware device. Raised the key incentive question: if there's an edge/alpha in *not* conforming to a rule set, what incentivizes standardization at all? From an efficiency view, the "optimal" result would be everyone adopting the most efficient method (e.g., courtroom stenographers' approach), but that no longer makes sense given voice dictation.
  • **How to unseat an incumbent standard (rafa's prompt):** rafa referenced Sachin's writing on an **annealing process** — you must inject as much energy as inertia has consumed to reset a standard.
  • **Has QWERTY's lock-in argument aged badly? (Sachin):** The 1985 paper turned out to be substantially wrong in the long run — QWERTY is no longer a contested space. Alternatives abound: regional-language keyboards (type in English, convert to regional), voice notes, and touchscreens. He framed QWERTY as an **"ossified chain"** that AI and iPhone touchscreens "liberated," creating new standards for both keyboards and how we talk to computers.
  • **Voice, LLMs, and generational shifts:** Sachin uses voice-to-text heavily for coding/writing to ramble then narrow ideas; people are less attached to typing than before. rafa noted voice adoption only went exponential *with* LLMs — voice alone hadn't gotten there — and that the taboo around voice has decreased. He observed his own household inverts the norm (kids napping makes *talking* the contested space, so text dominates), and that his nephews browse via voice and touch, rarely typing. Andre countered that voice notes require a closed door, which clashes with open-office environments.
