ProtFiSIG February 26, 2026: Network Realism Discussion

Definition and characteristics of Network Realism genreAnalysis of serialized fiction format and pacing concernsThe challenge of near-future fiction becoming outdated by realityUse of vagueness and empty space as literary techniqueScience fiction as commentary on present rather than prediction of future

Participants: sachbenny, drevius., randylubin, suspencer_nit, 3221229683, thewanderingeditor, amitashu

The ProtFiSIG group gathered to discuss Network Realism, a genre defined by James Bridle as fiction set in the near future with networked components that blur the line between fiction and present reality. Members analyzed a serialized story, appreciating its use of second-person perspective and evocative technical details, but noted that the continuous format became fatiguing and could be improved through condensing by approximately 30% and spacing releases. The discussion highlighted a central tension in near-future fiction: the risk that advancing technology renders the narrative predictions obsolete, as evidenced by Charlie Stross's documented struggles with the genre. The group explored how deliberate vagueness and empty narrative spaces actually strengthen Network Realism by allowing readers to project contemporary context onto the work. A key insight emerged that science fiction functions primarily as commentary on the present rather than literal prediction of the future, which reframes Network Realism's purpose and value. The meeting concluded with positive feedback about the story selection and emerging interest in developing asynchronous participation options to accommodate members unable to attend live sessions.

  • Network Realism is defined as fiction set in the near future with networked components that samples real-world artifacts, creating a 1:1 relationship between fiction and present reality.
  • Participants found serialized fiction formats engaging but potentially fatiguing, with suggestions that condensing content by 30% and spacing releases over 10 days could improve reader experience.
  • The genre faces an inherent challenge: as demonstrated by Charlie Stross's experience, rapidly advancing technology can quickly overtake near-future narrative predictions, making the work feel dated.
  • Deliberate vagueness and empty space in Network Realism stories function effectively to allow readers to fill gaps with contemporary context, extending the work's relevance.
  • Science fiction fundamentally operates as present-day social commentary rather than literal future prediction, which is central to understanding Network Realism's purpose and appeal.
