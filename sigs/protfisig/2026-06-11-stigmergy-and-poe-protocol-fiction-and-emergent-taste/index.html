Stigmergy and Poe: Protocol Fiction and Emergent Taste

Stigmergy as a literary concept in Edgar Allan Poe's fictionThe Pit and the Pendulum as an example of stigmergic storytellingPoe's essay on furniture and taste as environmental emergenceThe Jamverse shared universe project and websiteStigmergy as a cross-institutional topic at the Protocol Institute

Participants: suspencer_nit

The ProtFiSIG group convened to explore stigmergy through the lens of Edgar Allan Poe's literary and philosophical work. The session examined how Poe employs stigmergic principles in his fiction, particularly in The Pit and the Pendulum, where indirect coordination and environmental cues drive narrative tension and meaning. Additionally, participants reviewed Poe's essay on furniture and taste, analyzing how his ideas about the emergent nature of aesthetic preferences align with stigmergic theory—the concept that complex behaviors arise from simple environmental interactions rather than centralized control.

As a practical component, the group tested the new website for the Jamverse, an experimental shared universe being developed by the Protocol Institute. Sachin presented the technical infrastructure he built and announced an upcoming contest that would invite ProtFiSIG members and the broader community to contribute to the Jamverse. This integration of theoretical exploration and practical application exemplified the institute's approach to protocol fiction.

  • Poe's work demonstrates how stigmergy—indirect coordination through environmental modification—operates in narrative structure and thematic development.
  • Poe's thinking about taste and furniture reveals how aesthetic preferences emerge environmentally rather than through direct prescription.
  • The Jamverse project applies protocol fiction principles to create a shared universe with community participation through an upcoming contest.
