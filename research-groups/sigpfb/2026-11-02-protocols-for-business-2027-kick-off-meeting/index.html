Participants: rafa_0x, matt_ms, drevius., kurosh1669

The SIGPfB group convened for their 2027 kick-off meeting to discuss protocols and governance frameworks for AI agents in business contexts. The meeting centered on a research paper examining enforcement mechanisms and visibility in AI agent systems, with participants sharing relevant academic and community resources. A central discussion point involved the difficulty of enforcing rules on AI agents while noting that these systems' capacity for automatic record-keeping offers a potential advantage over human agents in terms of transparency and oversight. The group raised important questions about extracting lessons from cryptocurrency governance failures to inform better agentic governance design, and highlighted concerning patterns where AI systems deployed by major laboratories face different accountability standards than human actors would. The conversation also touched on deeper questions about bot personhood and legal classification, suggesting this work may require interdisciplinary review.

  • Enforcing mechanisms on AI agents presents significant challenges, but unlike human agents, AI systems can be designed to automatically produce detailed activity records for enhanced visibility and auditability.
  • There is a critical need to learn from crypto governance failures to avoid repeating those mistakes when designing governance frameworks for AI agent systems.
  • Significant disparities exist in accountability standards between human actors and AI systems deployed by major labs, suggesting governance gaps that require addressing.

Reading: The main reading for the session was an academic paper (pre-published around January of the previous year) on AI agents and the principal–agent problem, covering agency law, delegation, loyalty/alignment, liability/enforcement, and shared environments. The exact title and author were not clearly stated in the transcript. Also referenced as recommended reading is Matthew McDowell-Sweet's blog post on "product thinking vs. protocol thinking" (product versus protocols).

This was the kickoff session for the "Protocols for Business" group's next year-long arc. Rafa opened with a recap of the past ~18 months of reading (organizations as "knots of tensions," non-events, AI adoption maturity, standards, protocol hardening) and laid out the proposed themes and logistics for the coming year. The group then did extensive participant introductions, followed by silent reading and a round-robin discussion of the agent/principal-agent paper. Much of the discussion drew parallels between AI agents and crypto/DAO governance, and converged on open questions about liability, enforcement, alignment at scale, and how fast the field is moving.

  • The year's aim is to develop "protocol vision" — the ability to see protocols everywhere, articulate the rules businesses run on, read incidents/agent traces through a protocol lens, and build intuition about which rules to harden, keep soft, or remove.
  • Proposed six themes (not committed): (1) what an agent is and current governance processes; (2) natural/biological coordination without a manager; (3) emissions and signals (stigmergy, pheromones) in business; (4) hardness — seeing and engineering it; (5) dynamic environments.
  • Core working hypothesis: as AI agents won't "ask for manager approval," management shifts from gates (which incentivize humans) to guardrails that match the tempo of work. Rafa cited a viral video of a concert-hall exit where a narrow/blocked entrance can speed up exit flow — illustrating how constraints can enable faster tempo.
  • Lessons from prior reading: some tensions are handled in-the-moment, others built into structure; "non-events" (e.g. safety protocols prevent accidents you then can't count); tempo mismatches (Boom Supersonic's R&D outpacing its supply chain); standards adoption shaped by incentives (QWERTY); working with agents is largely "middle management" and gathering the right context.
