SIG-MRG 2026-10-08

Participants: Kei 🗝, hyperstitious, Jeremiasz Rzenno | CET, Mark Elder, Othmangba, Ben, jawn

Kei framed the session as building on a prior discussion about the origin of Levin's bioelectric/morphogenic patterns, shifting focus to the nature of those patterns and their proposed agency and "evolution" within a quasi-neoplatonic realm. After a silent reading of an excerpt, participants shared what felt intuitive versus shocking in the paper, with particular attention to Levin's blending of hard biology with psychoanalysis and platonic metaphysics. The conversation ranged across memory models (storage/retrieval vs. distributed/dynamic), the question of whether a separate nonphysical realm exists, and connections to ethics, Giordano Bruno, and embedding spaces.

  • Kei (framing): Levin posits that morphologies "ingress" into the world from a nonphysical, quasi-neoplatonic space — a rereading of Plato's realm of forms, where forms may be not just geometric (triangles) but lifelike, with their own agency and even their own evolution.
  • Kei (caveat): Though Levin is a rigorous biologist at Tufts, this paper is speculative/intuitive, published in a philosophy journal, and not necessarily well received among his scientific peers.
  • Opening question (wound healing / autonomous processes): Kei asked what the body "consults" when it heals a wound overnight or breathes autonomously; his intuitive answer was "cellular memory," but what is cellular memory? Mark: Connected this to autogenic training — consciously controlling normally unconscious processes (e.g., sending blood to fingertips). Kei: Mentioned a study showing imagined muscle contractions increased muscle mass beyond the norm. Othmangba: Suggested such memory may be largely stored in DNA — genetic/epigenetic instructions the body defaults to. Kei noted Levin's claim is more radical than epigenetics: an actual nonphysical realm of ingressing forms.
  • Levin's distinction (via Kei): Different sciences are different tools; physics "lacks explanatory power" for things like habit, OCD, or mind — you wouldn't bring a hammer to a glassblowing workshop. Levin situates minds along a spectrum of complexity/agency (his "diverse intelligences" program), not panpsychism.
  • Othmangba: The low-/high-agency patterns in platonic space reminded him of habit formation — an idea begins as a thought, enters "meat space," and becomes unconscious behavior.
  • hyperstitious (Martina): Found the paper both intuitive and shocking given its hard-science lineage. Noted its resemblance to theories of computation/mind and to rationalist language; was struck by Levin describing this as a behavioral science, with a diagram placing mathematics at the bottom and psychoanalysis at the top, plus repeated references to Carl Jung.
